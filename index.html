THR

Thermal-Headroom Routing

In the desert heat, a robot's detector choice now decides how slow its chip gets a minute later. THR checks the temperature forecast first.

A small embedded chip running the biggest detector on every frame hits its thermal limit in about a minute at 45 °C. The governor then down-clocks it and every model slows down, the small ones included. THR forecasts the chip temperature in closed form and skips any configuration that would cross the throttle line. Each decision takes about 7 µs.

The problem

In hot air the router causes its own slowdown.

Adaptive inference picks a model size and resolution per frame to meet a deadline. Most routers watch latency and back off when it rises. On a hot chip that signal shows up after the damage is done.

Reactive · watch latency, then back off

Belief-driven routing

Tracks a belief over compute contention from recent latencies. It keeps blackouts near one second, but by the time latency jumps the governor has already throttled. At 45 °C it spends 11 to 13% of each mission throttled, and 32 to 38% at 55 °C.

→ responds after the chip is hot
Forecast · check headroom before choosing

Thermal-Headroom Routing

Reads the on-die temperature, predicts where an RC thermal model says the chip will be 5 s from now under each configuration, and drops any that would cross 88 °C. The cheapest model is always allowed, so the router never gets stuck.

→ 0% throttled at 45 and 55 °C
What we measured

Seven traces, eight temperatures, twenty seeds.

Closed-loop simulation driven by real camera traces from TartanDrive (off-road), RADIATE (rain, snow, fog, night) and WoodScape (fisheye soiling), with real profiled latencies. Every comparison is a paired test over 20 seeds, Holm-corrected across the seven traces, against baselines picked to beat THR. The results hold, with larger effects, when every decision is given one deadline period of wall-clock time.

Throttling · 45 and 55 °C
0% of the mission, on every trace and seed

The belief router is throttled 11 to 13% of the time at 45 °C and 32 to 38% at 55 °C.

Deadline misses · vs. belief router
3.9 to 7.5 pp fewer (7 of 7 traces, Holm)

3.9 to 5.4 points at 45 °C and 6.7 to 7.5 at 55 °C, with the deadline at the big model's median latency. Energy per on-time frame drops 12 to 13%.

A chip 20% hotter than assumed · 55 °C
THR 0% throttled, thermostat 29%

A thermostat at 88 °C works on the chip it was set for. When the real chip runs hotter or heats faster, it overshoots and throttles. Given each chip's heating parameters (one 7-minute heating curve to measure), THR looks 5 s ahead and stayed at zero on every chip variant we tried.

Simple rules, one ambient, known chip
Same trade-off curve as THR

A thermostat or duty cap at the right setting lands on the same accuracy-timeliness curve, because the share of big-model frames sets the outcome. A cap tuned at 45 °C then throttles 16 to 31% of the time at 50 to 55 °C.

Deadline with 25% slack
Belief router wins on on-time utility

With slack, the small models still finish on a throttled chip, so throttling costs little and THR's rationing costs more accuracy than it saves (6 of 7 traces).

Always run the big model · 55 °C
91 s blackout, 88% of deadlines missed

At 45 °C the static heavy policy is throttled 20% of the time and goes 12.5 s without an on-time detection.

What it costs: THR serves the big model less often, so pseudo-ground-truth agreement at 45 °C falls to 0.950, against 0.975 for the belief router. Below 35 °C it makes exactly the same choices as the baseline. We picked the thermal parameters; heating measured on an Apple M5 under nonstop YOLO11 fits the same model within 1 °C, with a 20 s time constant and 2.8 °C/W against our assumed 30 s and 2.0 °C/W. The M5 never throttled, and the paper lists every other limitation.

Results integrity

How we kept ourselves honest.

  • Settings locked before the first run. Plant parameters, forecast horizon, margin, dwell and slow threshold were fixed in code beforehand. Each result file records eval_labels_used_for_selection: false.
  • Baselines built to beat us. A thermostat, duty caps tuned offline, and a control handed THR's own heavy-model budget. They match THR at one temperature on a known chip, and the paper says so.
  • Corrected statistics. 20 seeds per comparison, paired tests, and Holm correction across the seven traces of every family.
  • Cool air is a negative control. At 35 °C and below THR has to make the same choices as the baseline, and it does, to 0.0 pp on every trace.
  • Pseudo-ground truth is labeled as such. Accuracy is agreement with the largest model, which tracks real annotations poorly. Miss, throttle and energy numbers do not depend on it.
  • No hand-copied numbers. Every figure and table in the paper is generated from outputs/extreme_env/extreme_env.json by src/make_extreme_figures.py.
